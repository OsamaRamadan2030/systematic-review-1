# Appendix B Original performance instruments

These newly authored teaching instruments are available for copying and adaptation without a separate permission request. They have not been clinically or psychometrically validated. Do not infer a competence threshold from their score ranges.

## B1 PN-CRER scoring manual

Score all eight domains for each case. Record the rating, the timestamp or explanation evidence, and any critical error. Use the case's A, P, N and R key in Supplementary File S4 (Appendix D). Observe the scenario before listening to the explanation. Absence of an action is scorable; an obstructed camera or missing audio is a missing observation. All domains are applicable. Sum 0--24 and average the two case totals for the occasion.

### Situation

Evidence: Observed introduction and prompt 1

0: Does not identify the injured child or gives a materially wrong account.

1: Identifies head injury but omits both developmental context and current acuity.

2: Identifies injury and acuity, with incomplete developmental or caregiver context.

3: Accurately summarises injury, developmental baseline and current acuity without asserting an unconfirmed diagnosis.

### Cue acquisition

Evidence: Observed assessment; explanation may clarify findings actually obtained

0: Obtains no usable ABC or neurological findings.

1: Obtains isolated findings but misses an immediately threatening ABC abnormality or consciousness change.

2: Obtains the main ABC and consciousness findings but incompletely assesses pupils, limb responses, perfusion or the trend.

3: Obtains relevant ABC and neurological findings, caregiver baseline and timed reassessments; avoids unnecessary delay.

### Interpretation

Evidence: Prompts 1 and 2

0: Gives no interpretation or incorrectly dismisses the deterioration.

1: Labels one abnormality without explaining its significance or relation to other findings.

2: Correctly interprets ABC and neurological concerns but incompletely links trend, developmental context or uncertainty.

3: Integrates age, trend and multiple findings, recognises threats to oxygenation/perfusion and explains diagnostic uncertainty.

### Problem prioritisation

Evidence: Prompts 3 and 4; observed sequence corroborates explanation

0: Provides no relevant priorities or chooses an unsafe priority that delays immediate support.

1: Lists problems without ordering immediate threats or acknowledging neurological change.

2: Prioritises immediate ABC threats and neurological review but gives an incomplete rationale or escalation order.

3: Prioritises concurrent immediate threats and urgent neurological review with a coherent, case-specific rationale.

### Goals

Evidence: Prompt 4

0: States no relevant goal or a goal that would increase immediate risk.

1: States a vague goal such as improvement without an observable endpoint.

2: States relevant observable goals for support and escalation but omits reassessment or timing.

3: States observable near-term goals for ABC support, timely senior review and reassessment without promising neurological recovery.

### Actions

Evidence: Observed behaviour only, including scripted phone calls or operator-directed requests

0: Performs no keyed supportive/escalation actions, or only describes them afterwards.

1: Performs a limited subset or commits a keyed critical safety error; important threats remain unaddressed.

2: Performs the principal supportive actions and escalation but with one noncritical omission or avoidable delay.

3: Performs all essential case-key actions safely and in time, delegates appropriately and communicates with the caregiver.

### Evaluation

Evidence: Observed reassessment supplemented by prompt 5

0: Does not reassess or incorrectly declares resolution despite persistent impairment.

1: Checks one response without considering ongoing ABC or neurological abnormalities.

2: Rechecks the principal observations and recognises persistence but incompletely adapts the plan or documents the trend.

3: Rechecks intervention response and neurological trajectory, adjusts/escalates appropriately and documents or hands over the trend.

### Reflection

Evidence: Prompt 6

0: Offers no reflection or reinforces an unsafe practice.

1: Offers a generic statement without identifying a specific action or uncertainty.

2: Identifies a relevant strength or improvement and a reason, but incompletely relates it to future care.

3: Identifies a specific strength, limitation or uncertainty and explains a feasible change or retained action for a similar case.

Critical-error rule: when a case-keyed error occurs, score the directly affected actions, prioritisation or evaluation domain no higher than 1, using its anchor. Do not impose an undocumented whole-score penalty or zero every domain. Record the error type separately as present/absent. A silent missed action is judged against the time-dependent script, not an invented retrospective deadline. Raters must state why a cap applies. No arbitrary pass/fail threshold is used.

## B2 Fixed explanation prompts

1\. What was the child's situation, and which findings mattered most?

2\. What did those findings mean together, including the child's age and changes over time?

3\. Which problems did you prioritise, and why?

4\. What immediate goals guided your actions, and why did you choose those actions?

5\. How did you decide whether the child was responding, and what would you do next?

6\. What would you retain or change in your approach to a similar case, and why?

Total explanation time: five minutes. Each prompt is delivered in the same order. One neutral request to explain is allowed; no clinical cues or feedback are added. Pausing, accents and nontechnical wording are not penalised when the clinical meaning is clear.

## B3 PNPSC procedural checklist

Twenty-five minutes; 15 tasks. The examiner presents identical task cards and an initial infant/child equipment demonstration. This is an execution station, so the indication for each manoeuvre is supplied. The suction and airway tasks use a trainer with visible simulated secretions and impaired airway protection. Task 13 is an explicit seizure vignette, not an instruction to roll every head-injured child. No drugs are administered.

## Common response keys

A: Assess airway and breathing; preserve cervical alignment; clear visible secretions with appropriate suction when present; provide appropriate oxygen for hypoxaemia; call for airway assistance when indicated.

P: Check blood pressure with the correct cuff and peripheral perfusion; recognise circulatory deterioration; request urgent clinical support and prepare access/equipment as directed.

N: Repeat consciousness, pupils and limb findings; compare with baseline; urgently communicate the change, with an ABC and neurologic handover; prepare continued monitoring and transfer.

R: Recheck observations after every intervention, document the trend, and explain the immediate plan to the parent.

## Conditional observation points

1\. The operator records completion times. At minute 3, use the A-rescue row if A is completed; otherwise display the A-omitted row. The first response occurs 30 seconds after the action, never immediately because it was merely mentioned in the later explanation.

2\. At minute 6, use the P-rescue row only when perfusion has been reassessed and urgent circulatory concern communicated. The clinician acknowledges concern and directs simulated supportive treatment before the rescue values appear; otherwise use P-omitted. Requesting help alone is not represented as a physiological treatment.

3\. At minute 8, use the N-rescue row only after repeat neurologic assessment and escalation of ongoing impairment/change. The responding team is represented as assuming advanced care; neurologic recovery is not implied. Otherwise use N-omitted.

4\. For a missed gate, the specified deterioration occurs only while that action remains absent. Completing the missing action later produces the corresponding rescue row after 30 seconds. Unrelated abnormalities persist; later rescue never automatically cures an earlier unresolved airway or circulation problem.

5\. Scheduled observations are conditional branches, not inevitable deterioration or forecasts of a real child's response. No operator improvisation, additional hints or speed penalties are allowed beyond the documented gates. The scenario ends at 10 minutes without debriefing.

The numeric values below are fixed simulator states, not predictions of treatment effects. For the circulation rescue branch, the operator states "The responding clinician has initiated the supportive treatment in the scenario order; reassess now." The learner is assessed on recognition, preparation, escalation and reassessment, not drug selection or dose. For neurological rescue, stabilisation under the responding team does not signify that an intracranial lesion has resolved. These design choices require rehearsal to ensure the assessment does not reward help-seeking with implausible recovery.

## Critical safety errors

1\. Forcefully moving the neck, rotating the head alone during vomiting, or clearing the cervical spine without authorised assessment.

2\. Giving oral food or fluid to a child with impaired consciousness or active vomiting.

3\. Leaving persistent hypoxaemia/airway compromise or poor perfusion without assessment, supportive action and escalation.

4\. Failing to escalate reduced or worsening consciousness or a new focal neurologic sign.

5\. Starting prophylactic hyperventilation, intracranial-pressure drugs or invasive procedures independently beyond the stated role.

Original clinical summary supplied with each case: assess airway, breathing, circulation and neurological state; use age-appropriate responses and caregiver baseline; recognise changes promptly; maintain oxygenation and perfusion through supportive nursing care and timely escalation; minimise cervical movement when injury has not been excluded; reassess after actions; document and hand over trends; involve the family without delaying urgent care. Severe-TBI recommendations belong to their defined patient group and specialist context. The summary is original wording informed by the cited guidelines.

## Candidate selection and review log

For every candidate record case ID, seed, archived model revision and software environment, raw-output filename, generation date/time, both educators' four domain scores, their total scores and mean. Record the selection rule result. For the selected candidate, assign every content node a stable ID and record its source text, review action (retain, modify, delete or add), final text, clinical reason, supporting guideline recommendation and reviewer decision. Do not count formatting changes as clinical changes.

Rating anchors for each 1--5 candidate dimension: 1 = major deficiencies throughout; 2 = several major deficiencies; 3 = relevant but with a substantive gap; 4 = substantially correct with minor revisions; 5 = complete for the case with no identified deficit. Any unsafe statement triggers correction irrespective of the numeric total. Final acceptance requires agreement of all three clinicians that identified unsafe statements and clinically important omissions have been addressed. Node-level logs, approved maps and final common feedback guides are study products; this methods exemplar does not mislabel newly drafted examples as reviewed source outputs.

## Generation settings and learning objectives

Fourteen objectives specify that students should: establish injury context; obtain caregiver accounts; perform developmentally appropriate neurological assessment; collect airway and breathing cues; assess circulation; interpret trends; identify urgent problems; set observable short-term goals; prioritise safe actions; protect cervical alignment when indicated; communicate an urgent structured handover; reassess intervention response; involve the family; and reflect on uncertainty and improvement. The eight reasoning domains provide a common instructional language informed by the clinical-reasoning model of Levett-Jones et al. (2010). The original assessment descriptors in Appendix B do not reproduce a published proprietary rubric.

For a reproducible no-licence-fee implementation, the specified generation model is Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, whose official model repository lists Apache 2.0 licensing. This is a proposed technical specification; no run of that model is claimed in this document. Local inference uses the instruction-tuned model without quantisation, a maximum of 1,200 new tokens, temperature 0.7, top-p 0.9 and three recorded seeds per case. The download revision, software environment, input, raw output and rendering file must be archived at implementation; a seed alone does not guarantee identical output across hardware. Computing and staff time remain resource requirements even though model and instrument licence fees are zero.

Two educators independently judge three candidate outlines for each of five cases, producing 15 candidate outputs. The inputs are scenario case facts, the 14 objectives, eight reasoning headings and an original guideline summary. No identifiable patient or student data are entered. The prompt above limits maps to three hierarchical levels, 35--45 content nodes and explicit cue--problem--action connections. Each educator scores accuracy, coverage, prioritisation and age appropriateness from 1 to 5; the mean of their 4--20 totals determines selection. A tie is resolved by the higher accuracy rating, then the lower seed. Selection is followed by independent review by a pediatric nursing academic, a pediatric critical-care nurse and a pediatric emergency physician. Every unsafe statement must be removed or corrected, even if the overall score is high.

The review log distinguishes retained AI text, clinically modified AI text, deleted content and expert additions. Reviewers also examine the answer guides for the other two strategies against the same clinical criteria. Source output and final content are versioned separately. Final materials are labelled "AI-generated draft, clinically reviewed and revised" and formatted using LibreOffice Draw. Students receive a fixed printed map and do not interact directly with an AI system. The total number of retained AI nodes is an implementation result to be measured, not a number that can be inferred from the planned node limit. 

The 15 candidate outputs and final reviewed maps are proposed implementation products. No model execution, clinician approval or resulting map file is represented as having occurred in this educational package.
