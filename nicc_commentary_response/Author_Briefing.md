# Author briefing: response to "Calibrated to what?"

**For the three authors only. Do not send this to the editors.**

## 1. What this is

- This is an ordinary scholarly commentary. It is **not** a complaint, a correction request or a misconduct concern. The journal is simply offering us the right of reply.
- The commentary is complimentary overall. It calls the study a source of "rare access" to how nurses use AI-EWS and agrees that silent override "deserves the governance attention the authors call for". It also calls our proposed override field "valuable".
- It does not question our design, sampling, ethics, analysis or the credibility of the themes. Its one concern is an "inferential step": moving from nurses' **accounts** of trust to the claim that their trust was **calibrated**, in the human-factors sense of matching the system's real reliability.
- Replying is optional. We recommend replying because a calm, well-referenced reply makes the paper look stronger, while silence can look like agreement.

## 2. Strategy of the response

The response is firm on substance and generous in tone. It makes one small concession on wording, which builds credibility.

| Commentary point | Our response | Where the paper supports us |
|---|---|---|
| Interviews cannot detect automation bias, which is a behavioural construct. | Agreed. We never measured or detected commission or omission errors. Automation bias was a *sensitising* lens, subordinate to Interpretive Description. | 4.2: "remaining subordinate to the inductive, interpretive commitments of ID" |
| "Calibrated trust" cannot be established from self-report. | We did not claim the match was established. We said so in the Limitations. Our participants described trust with high *specificity* (Lee & See's term). **Concession:** "calibrating" (the work) would have been more exact than "calibrated" (the outcome). | 6.2 Limitations: "considered self-understanding rather than … a verified description of in-the-moment behaviour" |
| Nisbett & Wilson: people lack access to what drives their judgements. | Their critique is about access to cognitive *processes*, not to experience and meaning. Nurses' meanings have consequences of their own: what they teach (P19), whether they document (P16), and how disagreement feels (P05, P09). | 4.6, Findings |
| Theory is "already dynamic" (Lee & See; Parasuraman & Manzey). | Agreed. Our claim was about how automation bias is framed in the *healthcare* literature. Lee & See's own model places trust within its organisational and cultural context, and our findings give that context nursing content. | 6.1 |
| Feedback asymmetry: false alarms are visible, misses are not. | We **welcome** this as an extension of our argument. P09, the commentators' own example, shows that a miss becomes visible through independent nursing surveillance. So the asymmetry *strengthens* our main recommendation that surveillance must be "deliberately taught, practised and protected". | 6.1 (surveillance, omission vulnerability) |
| Differentiated trust may reflect algorithm aversion. | Our data show institutional pressure **towards** reliance: P17 still escalated an artefact alarm, and P09 said "the algorithm becomes the standard". Silent override was an assessed judgement, not "disregard" (P16). | Themes 1.2, 2.3, 3.1, 4.3 |
| No local performance estimates; the Epic sepsis model example. | We acknowledged that proprietary details were not audited. The Wong et al. study concerned a *different, sepsis-specific* model, whereas ours is a general deterioration model, so its findings cannot be transposed. We agree on the principle that local performance should be known, and known to nurses. | 4.3 |
| Symmetric log-based capture; nurse-facing performance reports that include misses. | **Accepted** as a refinement of our override field. Our paper already warned against making the algorithm the "default standard". | 6.3 |
| Recommend behavioural designs. | Already recommended in our paper: observational and think-aloud studies, and linking EHR audit data. The two kinds of evidence are complementary. | 6.4 |

## 3. Safeguards built into the response

- It contains **no new data**. Every participant quotation and every phrase quoted from our paper was checked character by character against the published PDF; all 17 match.
- It cites **nine references**. All are well-established, and their details were checked: Thorne 2016; Blumer 1954; Lee & See 2004; Nisbett & Wilson 1977; Kahneman & Klein 2009; Tanner 2006; Wong 2021. The other two are our article and the commentary.
- It does not claim any triangulation, audit or outcome data that we do not have.
- It names no commentator and contains no criticism of them personally.
