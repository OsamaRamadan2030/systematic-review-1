# 1. Bottom line

No data review can make the submitted Results publishable as observed findings, because the uploaded package states that the numbers were generated, not collected. No raw student-level data were uploaded. The manuscript's disclosures about provenance therefore cannot be removed. What can be checked is listed below: the arithmetic reconciles, the planning calculations reproduce, and several internal inconsistencies and an instrument flaw need fixing whatever the data's origin.

The Results section reports as completed several events that S6 and the Methods say did not occur (the generation run, the clinician review, and the answer-guide review). The Results also rely on the same generated numbers that S7 describes as synthetic.

# 3. Why the disclaimers cannot be removed

Removing the provenance statements would make the manuscript claim collected data where the supplied files say the values were generated by code. That would misrepresent the data's origin to the editor and to readers. The user's statement that the data are theirs and were collected is not something these files support, and no raw records were supplied to show it. If the study was run, the correct step is to replace the simulated text with a description of the real study, built from the real records. That requires the records, which are listed in Section 9.

# 5. Inconsistencies to fix whatever the data's origin

1. **Missing-data sensitivity reference.** The zero-shift reference is given as 2.30 for the AI-arm shifts, 2.33 for the student-arm shifts and 2.32 for the multiple-imputation estimate. There should be one reference value.
2. **Benchmark count.** The manuscript says benchmarks occur after every 40 recordings. Each rater scores 180 recordings (120 case and 60 procedural), which implies about four benchmarks per rater, not 21. The reported 63 checks and 78 recordings do not fit the stated rule.
3. **Follow-up interval.** The text says performance assessment falls 12–25 days after the last teaching session. The schedule (29 March–9 April) gives 12–23 days from 17 March. The figure depends on the section, and the text should state this.
4. **Table numbering.** "Table 1" is used for both the instructional activities and the baseline characteristics. "Table 2" and "Table 3" are each used twice. Figure 1 is a text table, not a figure.
5. **Missing supplementary files.** The text cites S7, S8 and S9. S8 (attrition by arm) and S9 (18 MNAR outputs) were not supplied. S7 is synthetic.
6. **Calendar.** The schedule runs from January to April 2026. Today is 10 October 2026. A prospectively registered protocol with these dates would be in the past, and the text calls the schedule "illustrative." If the study was run, the dates must be the real ones.
7. **Ethics approval.** EC-2025-089 cannot be checked from these files. The approval letter is needed.
8. **Sample-size prior.** The planning standard deviation of 5 is not supported by the observed pooled SD of about 3.0. The power statement should use the observed SD or be presented only as a planning assumption.
9. **Title.** The title says "AI-generated versus student-generated," but the arms also differ in learner activity (examining and revising versus constructing). The title and abstract should describe instructional strategies.

# 6. Instrument review: knowledge keys

- **Option-length cue.** In S3, the keyed answer is the longest option in 39 of 48 items (81%). Chance is 25%. Test-wise students can score above chance by choosing the longest option, so the knowledge score is partly a test-wiseness measure. Option lengths should be balanced before any use, and the items should be reviewed by clinicians and a measurement specialist.
- **Key balance.** Each form has six keys of each letter, as stated.
- **Psychometrics.** S3 states that no psychometric validation has been done. KR-20 values and difficulty ranges in S7 come from generated responses, so they are not evidence for the items.

# 8. Literature the manuscript should engage

Found in the search and absent from the manuscript:

- Schroeder et al., 2018, meta-analysis of concept maps: creating maps had a larger effect (g = 0.72) than studying them (g = 0.43). This favours the student-constructed arm and must be discussed.
- Szydlak et al., 2025, Medical Teacher: ChatGPT-generated concept maps matched expert maps on clinical accuracy but lacked prioritisation and abstraction. Directly relevant to the AI-map arm.
- Sutthiviriwan et al., 2026: AI-assisted concept mapping in orthopaedic nursing education (quasi-experimental).
- Sawalha et al., 2026, BMC Medical Education: systematic review of mapping-based teaching in pediatric nursing education.
- Blunt & Karpicke, 2014, and Lenski et al., 2024: retrieval-based versus study-based concept mapping. This bears on whether the student-map arm's construction conditions were retrieval or elaboration.
- Xu et al., 2024: concept maps combined with case-based learning and flipped classroom.
- Niu et al., 2026: mind mapping combined with case-based learning using simulated cases.
- Meşe et al., 2026: ChatGPT conditions showed no advantage in nursing diagnostic skills. Balances the AI-positive literature.

# 9. What is needed to go further

To move from this audit to a real data review and analysis, provide:

1. The de-identified student-level dataset, one row per student, with section, facilitator, strategy, day, start time and learning group.
2. Baseline and follow-up case domain scores (8 domains × 2 cases × 2 occasions), with rater ID and critical-error flags.
3. Procedural task scores (15 tasks × 2 occasions), with rater ID.
4. Knowledge responses (48 items, with form and sequence).
5. Attendance, preparation and product logs per session; fidelity records; exposure questionnaire responses.
6. Rater benchmark and recalibration logs.
7. The ethics approval letter (EC-2025-089), the consent form and the allocation log, dated before recruitment.
8. The map-generation logs, if the AI maps were produced: model revision, prompts, raw outputs, the two educators' scores, and the clinician review log.

With the dataset, I will run the pre-specified mixed ANCOVA (Kenward–Roger) and sensitivity analyses in R, check the output against the reported values, and rebuild the manuscript's Results, tables and provenance statements from the actual output. Without the dataset, the honest form of the manuscript is a study protocol with no Results.
